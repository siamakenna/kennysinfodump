01 / PERTURB-LMBENCHMARK DEVELOPMENT · ONGOING

FROM PASSION PROJECT → RESEARCH BENCHMARK

Perturb-LM

Can biological language help us search cellular morphology?

Perturb-LM started with a question I couldn't stop thinking about: could biological language help us search cellular morphology? What began as a small passion project became a harder research question: not simply whether retrieval could work, but whether it worked for the right reasons.

I'm building a leakage-aware benchmark for natural-language retrieval of Cell Painting morphology. The work now includes explicit information boundaries, held-out evaluation, comparator models, uncertainty analysis, reproducibility documentation, a collaborator workflow, public research artifacts, and conference acceptances.

Project evolution / six steps
  1. 01 · QUESTION

    Can biological language retrieve meaningful cellular morphology?

  2. 02 · EARLY PROTOTYPE

    Text-to-morphology retrieval experiments.

  3. 03 · HARDEN THE QUESTION

    Identifier stripping, leakage controls, plate/well exclusions, held-out evaluation, and evaluability accounting.

  4. 04 · COMPARATOR SUITE

    TF-IDF, BiomedBERT, MedCPT, BioLORD, and CellCLIP.

  5. 05 · REPRODUCIBILITY

    Frozen benchmark definitions, metadata-role auditing, bootstrap uncertainty, versioned artifacts, and public-safe outputs.

  6. 06 · OUTSIDE THE REPO

    NECB 2026, Central US SynBio acceptance, collaborator workflow, and ongoing manuscript / benchmark development.

From biological query to retrieval test

  1. 1. Biological queryDescriptive metadata only; direct identifiers and acquisition fields excluded.
  2. 2. Text representationIdentifier-stripped TF-IDF or frozen BiomedBERT embeddings.
  3. 3. Train-only alignmentA ridge projection learned on training data maps BiomedBERT to morphology space.
  4. 4. Retrieval evaluationHeld-out plate / treatment tests with leakage-aware exclusions. The workflow snapshot uses 904 morphology features.

02 / TMEM106B NEURON ALIGNERCURATED REVIEW · METHOD VALIDATION

MICROSCOPY → QUANTITATIVE PHENOTYPING

TMEM106B Neuron Aligner

Longitudinal single-neuron tracking + phenotype analysis

I build and audit the workflow that follows neuron identity across imaging visits, then measures the 561 / mCherry phenotype within a fixed region. Neuron identity is established from 488 morphology rather than the phenotype signal being measured, keeping tracking and phenotype interpretation conceptually separate.

  1. 488 MORPHOLOGYNeuron identity + longitudinal tracking
  2. FIXED PHENOTYPE ROI561 / mCherry phenotype
  3. MANUAL SINGLE-NEURON QCReview before interpretation

Tight longitudinal 488 identity core · fixed phenotype ROI · manual single-neuron QC. The three matched well pairs, not the individual neurons, define the biological replicate structure.

In review: candidate tracks require curated acceptance before phenotype interpretation. These examples do not establish tracking accuracy, a TMEM106B effect, or lysosomal rupture.

HOW I WORK

notes to carry into the lab

01

Measure carefully

Biology gets messy quickly. QC, metadata, acquisition context, and experimental details are part of the result.

02

Make the pipeline auditable

Keep assumptions, exclusions, uncertainty, and preprocessing visible enough that another researcher can reproduce or challenge the conclusion.

03

Leave the project easier

Write the documentation. Organize the handoff. Mentor the next person. Leave behind a workflow another researcher can actually use.
